# 9-7-26 · Thinking Outside the Box

The assignment was exactly what it sounds like: take a cardboard box and make something useful out of it. One box, no other materials worth mentioning, and the only real constraint was that it had to actually work — not just look like it would. I ended up with four builds, and the interesting part wasn't any single one of them. It was how differently I had to think about the same material each time.

## What Got Built

**Phone audio director.** The one I'm proudest of. I cut a slot into a box face sized to hold a phone upright with the speaker firing into the enclosure, then shaped the interior so the sound reflects forward instead of down into the counter. It's a passive amplifier — no power, no electronics, just geometry. The volume bump is real, and more importantly the audio stops sounding like it's coming from under the table.

**Cup sleeve.** A band of cardboard wrapped and sized to the exact circumference of a drinking glass. Insulates the hand, keeps condensation off the granite, and stays on without tape.

**Pencil organizer.** A small open-top box with pens and pencils held through it so they stand upright and separated instead of rolling loose across the counter.

**Doorstop.** The simplest build and the one that gets used most. Folded cardboard compressed into a wedge, jammed under the door edge. Cardboard turns out to be great at this — it deforms just enough to grip the floor instead of sliding.

## Lessons Learned

**Cardboard has a grain, and ignoring it costs you.** The corrugation runs one direction. Fold with it and you get a clean crease; fold across it and the panel crushes and loses all its stiffness. Once I started planning cuts around the flute direction, everything I built got noticeably stronger. The cup sleeve only holds its shape because the corrugation runs vertically.

**Measuring the object beats measuring the cardboard.** My first cup sleeve was cut to a number I estimated. It was loose. The version that worked came from wrapping a strip around the actual glass and marking where it overlapped. Same with the phone slot — I traced the phone rather than trusting a spec sheet thickness.

**Constraints did the creative work.** Being limited to one material forced me to solve problems with shape instead of parts. The audio director has no speaker because it can't have one, and that limitation is exactly what made it a more interesting build than if I'd been allowed to add electronics.

## What I'd Do Differently

I'd prototype the audio director's interior angle instead of guessing it once and committing. There's an optimal reflection angle and I almost certainly didn't hit it — I'd cut three inserts at different angles and actually compare them. I'd also test the doorstop on more than one floor type; it grips hardwood well, but I have no idea how it does on tile.
